## Overview

This repository serves as a growing portfolio of my independent work in investment research, financial analysis, valuation, and applied economics.

My objective is to examine businesses, industries, financial markets, and economic questions through a structured, evidence-based analytical approach. The work combines fundamental company research, financial statement analysis, financial modeling, economic reasoning, and quantitative methods to develop well-supported conclusions.

The portfolio is organized across several complementary research areas:

- **Equity Research & Valuation:** Fundamental analysis of publicly traded companies, their business models, financial performance, competitive positioning, operating drivers, and valuation.
- **Sector & Industry Analysis:** Research into industry structure, competitive dynamics, sector performance, economic cycles, capital allocation, and the factors influencing industry profitability and growth.
- **Economic & Financial Research:** Independent investigations into economic and financial questions using economic theory, data analysis, statistical methods, and econometric techniques.
- **Mergers & Acquisitions:** Hypothetical transaction case studies exploring strategic rationale, company valuation, transaction financing, synergies, and financial implications.
- **Leveraged Buyouts:** Hypothetical private equity case studies examining acquisition financing, operating projections, capital structure, debt repayment, and investment returns.

The portfolio is intended to evolve as new research questions, investment opportunities, and areas of analytical interest emerge.

**Underlying philosophy:**

Data → Observation → Interpretation → Implication → Judgment → Conclusion

For equity research, this process extends to investment thesis development, counterarguments, valuation, and the final research report.

### Research Approach

Economic and financial investigations generally follow a structured process:

1. Define the research question.
2. Review the relevant economic or financial framework.
3. Identify suitable data and variables.
4. Select an appropriate analytical or econometric methodology.
5. Estimate, test, and evaluate the relationships.
6. Interpret the results and acknowledge limitations.
7. Discuss the economic, financial, or policy implications.

The objective is to move beyond descriptive statistics toward meaningful, evidence-based explanations while distinguishing empirical findings from causal claims and unsupported interpretations.

## Research Methodology

My equity research process follows eight stages designed to connect historical financial evidence, business fundamentals, forecasting assumptions, and valuation.

| Stage | Research Focus | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Historical Financial Analysis | Understand the company's reported financial performance |
| 2 | Common-Size & Ratio Analysis | Evaluate profitability, financial structure, efficiency, and returns |
| 3 | Trend Analysis | Identify meaningful financial and operating changes |
| 4 | Business & Industry Research | Connect financial observations to business and industry economics |
| 5 | Driver Tree & Assumptions | Develop evidence-based, company-specific forecasting assumptions |
| 6 | Three-Statement Modeling | Translate operating assumptions into integrated financial projections |
| 7 | Valuation | Evaluate intrinsic value, market expectations, and alternative scenarios |
| 8 | Equity Research Report | Communicate the investment thesis, supporting evidence, risks, and valuation |

**Guiding principle:**

> Financial analysis should help discover the investment thesis. The valuation model should quantify the thesis, not manufacture it.

A central objective is to understand what market expectations imply, where an independent analytical view may differ, and why that difference matters financially.

The methodology is adapted for sector-level and economic research, where the research question, data, and analytical framework may require a different approach.

## Research Principles

The portfolio follows several guiding principles:

1. **Evidence before conclusions:** Research begins with reliable financial, economic, and industry information.
2. **Observation before interpretation:** Identifying what changed is distinct from explaining why it changed.
3. **Economic reasoning:** Financial and economic relationships should be supported by a coherent analytical framework.
4. **Drivers before forecasts:** Forward-looking assumptions should reflect the underlying factors influencing outcomes.
5. **Transparency:** Reported data, empirical findings, assumptions, and analytical judgments should be distinguishable.
6. **Critical evaluation:** Alternative explanations, counterarguments, and methodological limitations should be considered.
7. **Appropriate methodology:** Research methods should fit the question being investigated rather than follow a rigid template.
8. **Clear communication:** Findings should be presented concisely, with supporting evidence and acknowledgment of uncertainty.
